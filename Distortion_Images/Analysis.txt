We converted all sourced images to .ppm before use.

These were the distortions applied (we'll just talk about the ones that we used for presentation):
- Houses -> Low-contrast noise -> Gaussian filter.
- Eiffel Tower -> Guassian noise -> Sobel / Canny. 
- Starry Night -> Gaussian noise -> Texture segmentation.

Since this commentary primarily focuses on Sobel and Canny results + Texture Segmentation results, we'll briefly mention what we observed for the other distortion.

For the houses, we applied a low-contrast Gaussian filter. We found that the filter still worked 
well despite the noise, as the edges were blurred uniformly.

Moving on to the Eiffel Tower:

SOBEL

- Edge Quality
    Sobel only produces much higher overall intensity compared to Guassian + Sobel.
    The edges are much brighter, and easier to view,
    
- Continuity
    Both are rather comparable in continuity too. I don't notice too much of difference with or without the Gaussian filter.
    This may just be due to the fact that the original image was very high in contrast, so filter or no filter, the edge detection
    was still set up to work well.

- Noise Sensitivity
    Once again, both are not very sensitive to noise in the image. That being said, the original image already had heavy
    preprocessing, so our blurring may not have had as much of an effect as it was mitigated by the quality of the editing.

CANNY

- Edge Quality
    The edges are very similar in quality. Just Canny alone produces very thin and shaky edges (though not significantly, just in
    comparison).

- Continuity
    With the filter, there is a clear difference in continuity. You can see that the upper right tree leaves are able to be detected,
    whereas without the filter, they are absent.

- Noise Sensitivity
    The Eiffel Tower edges were much less noisy with the filter (you can look at the peak of the tower and see that along the edges,
    there are less stray lines being drawn).

Moving on to Starry Night Painting:

    Image segmentation was definitely impacted by Gaussian noise. This is because adding noise can simulate adding/changing
    textures in the image, and this is very visible in the results. The boundaries between different textures weakens in the
    image, resulting in the painting's feature distributions overlapping more.
